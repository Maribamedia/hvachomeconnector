FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most common questions about emergency HVAC repair.

What should I do if my furnace stops working? ▼
Check your thermostat settings and circuit breaker first. If it's still not working, call 1-800-555-0199 immediately. Our 24/7 team will dispatch a certified HVAC technician to your location.
How quickly can an HVAC technician arrive? ▼
Our average answer time is under 10 seconds, and a licensed HVAC technician is typically dispatched within 30–60 minutes of your call, depending on your location and the severity of the emergency.
Do you service all HVAC brands? ▼
Yes! Our technicians are trained and certified to repair, maintain, and install all major heating and cooling brands, including Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem, Goodman, and more.
Can you help with emergency AC repair in the summer? ▼
Absolutely. We provide 24/7/365 emergency air conditioning repair to ensure your home stays cool and safe during extreme heat waves.
How much does emergency HVAC repair cost? ▼
Costs vary based on the issue and parts required. Call our 24/7 line for a transparent, no-obligation diagnostic quote before any work begins.
Are your technicians licensed and insured? ▼
Absolutely. Every HVAC technician in our network is fully licensed, insured, bonded, and background-checked. We never send unqualified workers to your home.
What happens if I call outside business hours? ▼
We're open 24/7/365, including holidays. Our live emergency specialists are always on duty to dispatch an HVAC crew immediately, day or night.
Do you offer HVAC maintenance plans? ▼
Yes. We offer preventative maintenance plans to keep your system running efficiently, extend its lifespan, and prevent costly emergency breakdowns.
Can you fix both heating and cooling systems? ▼
Yes. Our certified technicians handle all aspects of HVAC, including furnaces, boilers, heat pumps, central air conditioners, and ductless mini-splits.
What should I do if I smell gas near my furnace? ▼
Evacuate the home immediately, do not flip any light switches, and call your gas company or 911 from outside. Once cleared, call us for an emergency furnace safety inspection.
